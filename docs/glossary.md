# IELTS Tracker Glossary

This document outlines key terminology, grading models, and rules used in the IELTS (International English Language Testing System) domain as implemented in the **IELTS Tracker** application.

---

## General Terms

### IELTS
**International English Language Testing System**. An international standardized test of English language proficiency for non-native English language speakers.

### Academic vs. General Training
The two streams of the IELTS exam:
- **Academic**: Intended for university admission and professional registration in English-speaking contexts. The reading passages and writing prompts have an academic context.
- **General Training**: Intended for migration purposes and secondary education. The reading and writing modules have a more practical, everyday context.

---

## Scoring Terms

### Band Score
The scale score of the IELTS exam, ranging from **1.0 to 9.0** in half-band increments (e.g., 6.0, 6.5, 7.0, etc.). A score of 0.0 is used if the candidate did not attempt the test.

### Raw Score
The number of questions answered correctly out of the 40 total questions in the Listening and Reading modules. Writing and Speaking do not have a raw score; they are graded directly on band score rubrics.

### Practice Set Status
A practice set is **Unstarted**, **To Practice**, **In Progress**, or **Completed**. Planning and starting are explicit study-list actions. Recording a linked attempt marks the set Completed; removing or moving its final attempt returns it to To Practice.

### Custom Book
A user-named source that groups custom practice sets. Custom books are identified explicitly in storage and do not use a reserved Cambridge volume number.

---

## Core Skills

### Listening
Consists of 40 questions spanning 4 sections. The Listening marking criteria are the same for both Academic and General Training streams.

### Reading
Consists of 40 questions. Academic and General Training Reading modules use separate conversion scales to map raw marks to band scores. The General Training scale has a slightly higher raw score threshold to achieve the same band score due to the simpler nature of the texts.

### Writing
Consists of two tasks (Task 1 and Task 2). Evaluated based on four criteria:
- Task Achievement/Response
- Coherence and Cohesion
- Lexical Resource
- Grammatical Range and Accuracy

### Speaking
A face-to-face interview consisting of three parts. Evaluated based on four criteria:
- Fluency and Coherence
- Lexical Resource
- Grammatical Range and Accuracy
- Pronunciation
